# Module 6 Practice: Emotion and Cognition

## Learning objectives{{attrs[#blk-49moyysldaty]}}

- **6.1a:** Compare explanatory questions and major families of emotion theory.
- **6.2a:** Evaluate what neural, physiological, expressive, and subjective measures can establish.
- **6.3a:** Explain conditional and bidirectional links between emotion, attention, and memory.
- **6.4a:** Distinguish interoception, appraisal, and labeling in subjective experience.
- **6.5a:** Assess how expression, context, culture, and language contribute to emotion perception.
- **6.6a:** Distinguish synchrony, shared causes, and interpersonal influence.
- **6.7a:** Design a valid evaluation of emotion inference in a new setting.

## Question 1 · Objective 6.1a{{attrs[#blk-d8367bwmbs0r]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 1
An emotion-related response appears in infancy. Does this establish its evolutionary function?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. The observation addresses developmental timing. It neither identifies the response’s adaptive consequences nor determines the contributions of inherited capacities and experience. A functional claim needs evidence about consequences in relevant environments; a mechanistic claim needs evidence about the processes producing the response.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 2 · Objective 6.1a{{attrs[#blk-8cooy3lmela7]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 2
Two theories both predict a fear report after a threat. What makes a stronger comparison than counting that result for both?
:::
::: tab Answer
Identify a manipulation on which they make different predictions, such as changing conceptual knowledge, appraisals, or opportunities for defensive action while specifying what each theory expects. Measure multiple relevant outcomes. Shared predictions provide compatibility, but a discriminating test requires a point of divergence.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 3 · Objective 6.2a{{attrs[#blk-lyqaaywggxbi]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 3
A wearable detects increased heart rate during a presentation. Which emotion can the researcher infer?
:::
::: tab Answer
Heart rate alone does not uniquely identify an emotion. The change could reflect several affective or nonaffective processes. Add contextual information, subjective reports, behavior, and other measures appropriate to the claim. Report increased heart rate directly instead of replacing the observation with an unsupported emotional label.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 4 · Objective 6.2a{{attrs[#blk-inqbn7mg57kq]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 4
A classifier distinguishes two emotion conditions in one laboratory. Name two tests required before claiming broad generalization.
:::
::: tab Answer
Test held-out people and meaningfully different tasks or settings, with labels obtained independently of the predictor. Examine performance across relevant populations and alternative states. Accuracy in a constrained comparison does not establish validity for unmeasured emotions or an unrestricted real-world setting.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 5 · Objective 6.3a{{attrs[#blk-6tlijueschbe]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 5
After an emotional film, participants recognize its central object better but remember the background worse. Is this contradictory?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. The outcomes concern different information. A selective-attention or binding account can predict benefits for some features and costs for others. Report item and contextual memory separately. The pattern is compatible with selective processing, but it does not establish the mechanism unless that process is measured or manipulated.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 6 · Objective 6.3a{{attrs[#blk-l04c1s0rpqcs]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 6
A person reports a vivid memory with high confidence. Which additional observation would directly test accuracy?
:::
::: tab Answer
Compare the remembered details against an independently established record, using a scoring procedure specified in advance. Confidence and vividness are features of the experience, not accuracy criteria. Avoid treating emotional intensity as evidence that a recalled detail must be correct.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 7 · Objective 6.4a{{attrs[#blk-swb7ctwcwvlh]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 7
Two students report the same bodily activation before presenting. One calls it excitement and the other anxiety. What information is missing?
:::
::: tab Answer
Assess appraisals, prior experience, perceived control, and the details and timing of bodily responses. Similar activation does not imply identical episodes, and different labels do not establish different physiology. The study should specify whether it explains felt experience, labeling, performance, or regulation.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 8 · Objective 6.4a{{attrs[#blk-ts14x8h9ajos]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 8
A labeling exercise changes emotion words but not behavior. Does it prove that labeling cannot regulate emotion?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. It shows a difference in the measured verbal outcome and no detected behavioral change under these conditions. Test uncertainty, manipulation strength, and the chosen outcome. It also does not prove successful regulation merely because participants used more precise words.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 9 · Objective 6.5a{{attrs[#blk-7w8ripdsb8nu]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 9
Observers label the same smile as friendly in a greeting and dismissive after a mistake. What does this suggest?
:::
::: tab Answer
Context contributes to interpretation; the facial movement alone did not determine the judgment. This does not show that the face conveys no information. Vary face and context independently to estimate their contributions, and include open-ended or naturalistic tasks if the claim concerns everyday understanding.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 10 · Objective 6.5a{{attrs[#blk-nd8iqtue20h5]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 10
An emotion-recognition task offers only four labels. Why might high agreement overstate what the task establishes?
:::
::: tab Answer
Agreement shows consistent selection among those options under the task conditions. It does not establish that observers would spontaneously use the same concepts, infer the expresser’s actual state accurately, or perform similarly in a different culture or context. The available alternatives constrain the inference.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 11 · Objective 6.6a{{attrs[#blk-x5d0o337xv7y]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 11
Roommates’ emotional responses become more similar over a semester. What comparison helps test interpersonal influence?
:::
::: tab Answer
Compare changes with an appropriate non-roommate group and document shared exposures, baseline similarity, and assignment processes. Random assignment to roommates can reduce selection concerns, but shared environments remain relevant. The result can support influence without identifying a unique mechanism such as mimicry or conversation.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 12 · Objective 6.6a{{attrs[#blk-p2zvmtb96u3u]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 12
Two participants show synchronized distress during a film. Can synchrony be scored as successful interpersonal regulation?
:::
::: tab Answer
Not without specifying the regulatory goal and measuring its achievement. The film may drive both responses, and matched distress may not be beneficial. Assess whether one person’s action changes the other’s state relative to a comparison and whether the recipient considers that change helpful.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 13 · Objective 6.7a{{attrs[#blk-0s267gi4se9d]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 13
An algorithm matches observers’ labels of posed faces 90% of the time. What is the strongest warranted description?
:::
::: tab Answer
It predicts observer labels with 90% accuracy in the tested posed-face task. The result does not establish 90% accuracy for private feeling, spontaneous expression, or a new population. Those claims require separate criteria and validation. The number is a hypothetical teaching example, not a reported finding.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 14 · Objective 6.7a{{attrs[#blk-a7zftuk3hcaf]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 14
Propose a study that distinguishes expression classification from inference about experienced emotion.
:::
::: tab Answer
Collect spontaneous expressions alongside independently obtained, time-matched experience reports and contextual information. Evaluate each target separately in held-out people and settings. Report discrepancies instead of treating the observer label and the person’s report as interchangeable ground truth. Consider whether the intended inference is feasible from the available signals.

**Source basis:** Niedenthal et al. (2025).
:::
::::
