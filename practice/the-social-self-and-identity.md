## Learning objectives{{attrs[#blk-57d5gd5f989q]}}

- **4.1a — Representation:** Explain how self-representation can be context-sensitive and stable.
- **4.2a — Social foundations:** Distinguish personal, relational, and collective self-representations without reducing culture to national categories.
- **4.3a — Processing:** Trace how information selection, interpretation, and memory influence self-judgment.
- **4.4a — Accuracy:** Distinguish rank-order accuracy from mean-level bias and evaluate self–other knowledge differences.
- **4.5a — Self-esteem:** Separate self-esteem level, scope, timescale, stability, and contingency when evaluating evidence.
- **4.6a — Functions:** Compare proposed functions of self-esteem and distinguish narcissism from self-compassion.
- **4.7a — Motives:** Differentiate self-enhancement, self-verification, and self-improvement using competing predictions.
- **4.8a — Integration:** Evaluate self-judgment biases and design a test of feedback use that separates feelings from change.

## Question 1 · Objective 4.1a{{attrs[#blk-yb1mbqb63hxm]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 1
A student is reserved in unfamiliar meetings but consistently outspoken in a familiar reading group. Explain how this could support stability rather than contradict it. What evidence is missing?
:::
::: tab Answer
Distinguish a stable average from a stable conditional pattern. Repeated reserved behavior with unfamiliar evaluators and repeated assertiveness in familiar groups could reflect predictable context sensitivity. A working self-concept account proposes that different cues activate different stored self-aspects. Repeated observations are necessary: two isolated episodes cannot establish a signature. Also measure opportunities to speak and evaluation pressure; behavior could differ because the situations permit different actions, even without a change in self-representation.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 2 · Objective 4.1a{{attrs[#blk-p95dhk5dtvid]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 2
A person confidently describes the same personal strengths every year, but work samples contradict the description. Distinguish clarity, continuity, and accuracy in this case.
:::
::: tab Answer
Confidence and consistency can indicate perceived self-concept clarity. A felt connection across years concerns continuity. Neither validates the content. Accuracy requires a defensible criterion matched to each claimed strength; work samples may supply one if they represent the relevant skill and context. The description could be clear and continuous while inaccurate. Alternatively, the samples could be too narrow. A strong answer evaluates the criterion instead of assuming that any external measure must be correct.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 3 · Objective 4.2a{{attrs[#blk-2b5rd7hfhrdp]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 3
Give personal, relational, and collective interpretations of a graduate student’s pride in a successful team project. Can all three apply?
:::
::: tab Answer
Personal pride could concern the student’s problem-solving skill. Relational pride could concern being a dependable collaborator for a particular partner. Collective pride could concern the laboratory or community with which the student identifies. All can apply, because these are levels of representation available within a person. Ask which meaning is salient under each cue, and measure it rather than assigning the student to a single type. The same observed pride cannot establish the level responsible.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 4 · Objective 4.2a{{attrs[#blk-j6sj80i743sm]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 4
An investigator compares students from two countries and attributes their average participation difference to independent versus interdependent selves. Identify two inferential problems and a better design.
:::
::: tab Answer
Country membership does not measure self-construal, and a difference in participation may reflect language, institutional norms, opportunity, or evaluation concerns. Broad independence/interdependence also combines dimensions that can vary separately. Measure the proposed dimensions, sample variation within each setting, and record relevant contextual constraints. A design that varies a theoretically relevant situational cue adds leverage, although a manipulation must be validated and cannot by itself explain a national difference. Interpret group averages without assigning them to every individual.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::

## Question 5 · Objective 4.3a{{attrs[#blk-agklnrkoguin]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 5
A recipient recalls positive feedback more accurately than negative feedback. Design a test of a threat-based memory account that does not confound threat with simple valence.
:::
::: tab Answer
Include feedback about central and peripheral self-aspects, with positive and negative versions matched in length, clarity, and exposure. Record immediate comprehension and delayed recall. A selective deficit for negative central feedback would better fit a threat-based account than an equal deficit for every negative item. A comparison with equivalent feedback about another person can help assess self-relevance. The design must still address baseline familiarity and differences in encoding; the result would support a mechanism under these conditions rather than a universal rule about criticism.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 6 · Objective 4.3a{{attrs[#blk-ab09bwq7hwpd]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 6
A presenter receives enthusiastic public comments, while private anonymous ratings are mixed. How could the public response maintain a self-view without establishing that observers share it?
:::
::: tab Answer
Public responses can reflect politeness, relationship maintenance, or cues about what the presenter hopes to hear. The presenter may then treat socially managed feedback as a direct expression of private evaluation. Compare the two measures within evaluators and examine how public feedback is interpreted by the recipient. Anonymous ratings also require scrutiny: they can introduce their own response tendencies. A discrepancy distinguishes communication from evaluation but does not uniquely identify politeness as its cause.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 7 · Objective 4.4a{{attrs[#blk-p379cfctz2nx]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 7
Hypothetical actual scores are 50, 65, and 80. Predicted scores are 60, 75, and 90. Calculate signed errors and mean signed error. Explain why rank-order accuracy and calibration yield different conclusions.
:::
::: tab Answer
Signed errors, defined as prediction minus actual score, are +10, +10, and +10. Their mean is +10 points. The ordering is exact, and the predictions are a constant shift of the criteria, giving a Pearson correlation of 1. Nonetheless, every estimate is too high. The association answers who performs better relative to whom; signed error answers whether the predicted values are calibrated. This constructed example supplies no empirical estimate of how accurate students generally are.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 8 · Objective 4.4a{{attrs[#blk-a6unss8hvm50]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 8
Hypothetical signed errors for four people are −12, −4, +4, and +12. Does zero average error establish that the people know themselves accurately?
:::
::: tab Answer
The mean signed error is zero because the negative and positive errors cancel. Mean absolute error is (12 + 4 + 4 + 12) / 4 = 8 units, revealing substantial individual error. Without the original predictions and criteria, rank-order association cannot be calculated. Report the unit, distribution of errors, and an appropriate association measure. Avoid moving from a group-level absence of directional error to a claim about each person’s knowledge.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 9 · Objective 4.4a{{attrs[#blk-nmblaxtfg9au]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 9
You want to assess private worry, conversational interruption, and perceived likeability. Which information sources would you seek, and why would agreement alone be insufficient?
:::
::: tab Answer
Private worry calls for the person’s report of experience, while recognizing that reporting conditions matter. Interruptions can be coded from sufficiently representative interactions; self- and informant reports can then be compared with those records. Likeability requires identifying whose evaluation is of interest and collecting it directly. Observability and evaluativeness motivate these choices. Agreement can reflect shared stereotypes or common method effects. Establish what each measure is intended to represent and whether it provides independent information rather than defining agreement as truth.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 10 · Objective 4.5a{{attrs[#blk-w1kwz8ucppdf]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 10
Design measurements that distinguish global trait self-esteem, current state self-esteem, academic self-evaluation, and academic contingency of worth.
:::
::: tab Answer
Use a measure of usual overall worth, repeated current evaluations at specified occasions, a domain-specific academic evaluation, and a measure of how strongly worth depends on academic outcomes. They address different questions and should not be substituted for each other. Couple daily reports with records of academic and nonacademic events to assess whether fluctuations track the proposed contingency. Do not reproduce scale items without checking their source and use requirements. A researcher should select validated instruments and justify their fit to the sample.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 11 · Objective 4.5a{{attrs[#blk-r1hemaj54m2o]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 11
A longitudinal study finds that self-esteem predicts later job satisfaction after adjusting for earlier satisfaction. State a supported conclusion and two reasons a causal claim remains qualified.
:::
::: tab Answer
The measured self-esteem score prospectively predicts later satisfaction conditional on the included adjustment. Unmeasured common causes may influence both variables, and participants may already anticipate changes that affect their current self-esteem. Measurement error and model specification can also matter. Temporal ordering is informative but not sufficient to show that raising self-esteem would raise job satisfaction. An intervention claim requires evidence about the intervention itself, its mechanism, and its effects on the relevant outcome.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 12 · Objective 4.6a{{attrs[#blk-4hyjg9pxxhin]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 12
Propose a study separating the sociometer and hierometer accounts without assuming that being respected and being included are the same experience.
:::
::: tab Answer
Independently vary feedback about competence-based standing and willingness of a group to include the participant, producing high/low conditions for each. Keep message strength and credibility comparable. Check perceived status and inclusion separately, then measure state self-esteem and relevant affiliative or assertive intentions. An inclusion effect with status held constant supports the acceptance-related prediction; a status effect with inclusion held constant supports the status-related prediction. Both could occur. Avoid claiming that a brief laboratory response proves the full evolutionary history proposed by either theory.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::

## Question 13 · Objective 4.6a{{attrs[#blk-kp0gttavakyv]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 13
Compare self-esteem, narcissism, and self-compassion using the response to a failed presentation. Why is a single response inadequate for diagnosis?
:::
::: tab Answer
Self-esteem concerns the evaluation of one’s worth; it can remain positive while a person recognizes a poor presentation. A superiority- or entitlement-based response illustrates features relevant to narcissism, whereas kindness, shared humanity, and balanced attention illustrate self-compassion. The constructs can overlap without being interchangeable. A brief response is context-dependent and does not establish persistence, impairment, or any diagnostic criteria. Assess later feedback use if the question is about learning, rather than drawing clinical conclusions about the presenter.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 14 · Objective 4.7a{{attrs[#blk-rxdotlj7w5sy]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 14
A student with a negative view of their quantitative ability chooses criticism over praise. Give enhancement, verification, and improvement interpretations and describe a discriminating test.
:::
::: tab Answer
Verification could favor criticism because it matches the prior belief. Improvement could favor useful criticism because it identifies a route to better performance. Enhancement could operate indirectly if receiving rigorous critique affirms the valued identity of being a serious learner. Separate favorability, consistency, and usefulness while holding credibility as constant as possible. Observe later corrective effort and performance as well as preference. No single choice uniquely identifies a motive, and combined motives should remain possible.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::

## Question 15 · Objective 4.7a{{attrs[#blk-ifungu483hhw]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 15
An upward comparison inspires one learner and discourages another. Explain the role of attainability without treating the comparison target’s success as sufficient to cause improvement.
:::
::: tab Answer
A successful target may provide a credible path forward when the learner sees relevant similarity, feasible steps, and resources to follow them. The same target may seem unattainable and heighten threat in another context. Measure perceived attainability, baseline skill, available support, and actual subsequent practice. Manipulating a feasible pathway independently of the target’s achievement would help test the account. Changes in inspiration are not equivalent to skill gains; retain a performance measure and acknowledge constraints beyond individual motivation.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 16 · Objective 4.8a{{attrs[#blk-ev1c0cwug34r]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 16
Most members of a class judge themselves below average at an unfamiliar juggling task. Why does this challenge a simple account in which all comparative judgments aim at self-flattery?
:::
::: tab Answer
A difficult task makes personal difficulty salient. If participants underweight the fact that others also struggle, they may rate themselves comparatively poorly. That pattern challenges an unconditional prediction of favorable comparative judgment. It does not show that motivation is irrelevant to all judgments. Measure absolute self-estimates, estimates for comparison targets, and a defensible performance benchmark. Varying task difficulty and providing distributional information can help test whether unequal information weighting contributes.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 17 · Objective 4.8a{{attrs[#blk-syraooc4caaa]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 17
Students predict what classmates think of them, and these predictions correlate with classmates’ ratings. Why is that not sufficient evidence of meta-insight?
:::
::: tab Answer
Predictions could come from projecting self-views that already correlate with classmates’ views. Measure self-views, metaperceptions, and classmates’ ratings separately; ask whether the predictions contain information about classmates’ ratings beyond self-views. Also consider whether students understand evaluator-specific differences, rather than merely their average reputation. Residual predictive value supports the stated operational test of meta-insight, but depends on reliable measures and a suitable model. A raw correlation alone does not distinguish the routes.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 18 · Objective 4.8a{{attrs[#blk-9db9yyg9dfqm]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 18
Design a brief experiment on self-compassion and feedback use. The reflection group feels better but does not produce better revisions. How should the study be interpreted?
:::
::: tab Answer
Randomly assign a compassionate or matched neutral reflection after the same substantive critique. Collect baseline task quality, a check of the intended response to difficulty, affect, feedback engagement, and blinded revision ratings. The described result supports a difference in measured affect, with no detected difference in performance. Report uncertainty rather than asserting exact equivalence. A mood effect is not evidence of improved reasoning, and a measured association between mood and revision does not establish mediation. Consider whether the task allowed enough time and whether the rating measure was sensitive before designing a follow-up.

**Source basis:** Chen and Critcher (2025).
