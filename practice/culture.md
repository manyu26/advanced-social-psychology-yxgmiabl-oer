# Module 7 Practice: Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology

::: info
Prepare a response before selecting **Answer**. Each section pairs a reinforcement problem with an interpretation or transfer problem. The worked responses model reasoning rather than a single required opinion. Scenarios and numbers are hypothetical teaching examples.

## Learning objectives{{attrs[#blk-hw5cspzi2y8u]}}

- **7.1a:** Explain culture without equating it with nationality or personal preference.
- **7.2a:** Evaluate sampling, measurement, and levels of analysis in cultural comparisons.
- **7.3a:** Distinguish kin, network, and category-based collectivism and their consequences.
- **7.4a:** Interpret cultural variation in self-representation and cognition without binary stereotypes.
- **7.5a:** Distinguish analytic–holistic orientations and tightness–looseness.
- **7.6a:** Explain how communication can maintain and transform cultural information.
- **7.7a:** Design a study of cultural change that separates practices, meanings, and causes.

## Question 1 · Objective 7.1a{{attrs[#blk-1ascu93vsbng]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 1
Two laboratories differ in whether junior researchers interrupt discussion. What would distinguish a cultural norm from an individual difference?
:::
::: tab Answer
Collect observations across members and situations, ask what participants expect and approve, and examine onboarding and responses to interruption. One outspoken individual is insufficient evidence of a group norm. Shared expectations and transmission practices support a cultural account, while individual preferences may still vary within each laboratory.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 2 · Objective 7.1a{{attrs[#blk-zn8voerb5cvm]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 2
A researcher labels one participant collectivist solely because of nationality. Identify the inferential error.
:::
::: tab Answer
A population label is being substituted for an individual measure. Measure the relevant orientation and local context instead. National averages, when valid, cannot determine an individual score; cultures also exist within and across national boundaries. The claim needs evidence at the level where it is made.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 3 · Objective 7.2a{{attrs[#blk-mitph2etgi0k]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 3
University samples in two countries differ on a self-construal questionnaire. What is needed before calling this a national difference?
:::
::: tab Answer
Assess whether the samples represent the populations, whether items function comparably, and which contextual differences accompany nationality. The direct result concerns those recruited samples and that instrument. Broader sampling and converging evidence can support broader claims, but a two-country comparison alone cannot isolate a cultural mechanism.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 4 · Objective 7.2a{{attrs[#blk-3mjjgvptow2w]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 4
Hypothetical schools average 4 and 6 on a seven-point cooperation scale. Can a student from the second school be assumed more cooperative than every student from the first?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. Group averages can differ while individual distributions overlap substantially. Report within-school variability and individual scores if the claim concerns a particular person. The two-point mean difference contains no information about complete separation and should not be turned into an individual stereotype.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 5 · Objective 7.3a{{attrs[#blk-7ks1bovz9ssv]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 5
A community provides extensive help to relatives but shows little trust toward unfamiliar residents. Is that inconsistent with collectivism?
:::
::: tab Answer
Not necessarily. Kin-based obligations and generalized trust concern different boundaries of sociality. Measure the target of help and trust rather than score the community on one undifferentiated collectivism scale. Also investigate institutions and opportunities for exchange with unfamiliar people.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 6 · Objective 7.3a{{attrs[#blk-983amgtm1gbo]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 6
How could a study test an honor-related explanation without assigning a fixed personality to an entire region?
:::
::: tab Answer
Measure relevant reputation concerns, perceived protection, and situational meanings within and across regions. Compare reactions to specified threats while considering other explanations. A regional label may guide sampling, but the explanatory claim concerns mechanisms and conditions that must be measured or manipulated.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 7 · Objective 7.4a{{attrs[#blk-7wknbk1r57am]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 7
A student emphasizes personal achievement in a job interview and family obligation in a community discussion. What would a contextual account predict?
:::
::: tab Answer
Different settings can make different self-representations relevant. Repeated observations should reveal systematic links with context rather than random switching. Measure what the student perceives each setting to require. The pattern does not establish that either description is false or that nationality uniquely explains it.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 8 · Objective 7.4a{{attrs[#blk-w9ej5ns14qtm]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 8
A published contrast is described as East versus West. What should a commentary ask before generalizing it?
:::
::: tab Answer
Identify the actual samples, tasks, languages, and measured dimensions. Ask whether the pattern appears within regions and whether alternative dimensions fit the evidence. Broad regional terms can summarize a comparison but cannot replace its sampling frame or establish that every member shares the average pattern.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 9 · Objective 7.5a{{attrs[#blk-t3vm7zihzlmc]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 9
One organization encourages independent initiative but strictly regulates safety procedures. Must it be classified as either individualist or tight?
:::
::: tab Answer
Those dimensions answer different questions and can coexist. Measure initiative-related meanings separately from norm strength and tolerance of safety deviations. Also specify the domain: strict safety rules do not necessarily imply strict norms for every aspect of organizational life.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 10 · Objective 7.5a{{attrs[#blk-wja82f5vjmkp]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 10
A cross-sectional study links perceived threat and norm enforcement. Why can it not establish that threat caused tightness?
:::
::: tab Answer
Temporal ordering is unknown, common conditions may affect both, and stronger enforcement may alter perceived threat. Historical, longitudinal, or experimental evidence can sharpen the claim, but each design needs its own assumptions. The association is consistent with a threat account without uniquely establishing it.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 11 · Objective 7.6a{{attrs[#blk-0p0gns6ooue6]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 11
A neutral story becomes more stereotype-consistent after several retellings. What would test the role of perceived shared beliefs?
:::
::: tab Answer
Vary information about the audience’s beliefs while keeping the original story constant, then compare changes across transmission chains. Measure what senders expect the audience to know. The pattern can test a communication mechanism; it does not show that every member personally endorses the stereotype.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 12 · Objective 7.6a{{attrs[#blk-w6gm2frbvgzm]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 12
A frequently shared claim is remembered well. Does widespread recall establish that the claim is true?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. Transmission success and factual validity are different outcomes. Repetition, emotional relevance, or perceived common ground can promote recall. Assess the claim against independent evidence and examine the communication process separately. A popular claim can be inaccurate, and an accurate claim can spread poorly.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 13 · Objective 7.7a{{attrs[#blk-0f1o7k1yb2vk]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 13
Unique first names become more common over decades. Can the researcher conclude that self-esteem increased?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. Naming is one indicator of practice and may have several explanations. Measure self-esteem directly with comparable instruments and samples if that is the claim. Historical co-movement would still require evaluation of shared causes and changing measurement conditions.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 14 · Objective 7.7a{{attrs[#blk-glsr5tnyui7l]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 14
Choose a self-serving judgment from Module 4 and design a culturally informative extension.
:::
::: tab Answer
Specify which self-domain and social audience matter, recruit across meaningful contexts, and measure relevant norms or self-representations. Test the proposed interaction instead of only comparing national means. Include a criterion for judgment accuracy so a difference in positivity is not automatically classified as a difference in bias.

**Source basis:** Kashima & Gelfand (2025).
:::
