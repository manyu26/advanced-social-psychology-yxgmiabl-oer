# Module 11 Practice: Prejudice, Discrimination, and Intergroup Relations

::: info
Prepare a response before selecting **Answer**. Each section pairs a reinforcement problem with an interpretation or transfer problem. The worked responses model reasoning rather than a single required opinion. Scenarios and numbers are hypothetical teaching examples. 
:::

## Learning objectives{{attrs[#blk-ab7c7p1ebjci]}}

- **11.1a:** Distinguish beliefs, evaluations, and treatment while identifying their levels of analysis.
- **11.2a:** Distinguish ingroup preference from outgroup hostility in group-based behavior.
- **11.3a:** Analyze how apparently helpful behavior can preserve unequal status.
- **11.4a:** Compare hierarchy maintenance with responses available to lower-status group members.
- **11.5a:** Explain how interpretations of history and threat can sustain conflict.
- **11.6a:** Match measures and comparisons to a bounded claim about intergroup behavior.
- **11.7a:** Evaluate contact using interaction quality, status, and consequences for inequality.

## Question 1 · Objective 11.1a{{attrs[#blk-tq8merb1edzz]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 1
A manager describes women as caring and assigns them support tasks instead of leadership opportunities. Identify the distinctions.
:::
::: tab Answer
The trait attribution is a stereotype; its positive tone does not establish equal treatment. The assignment pattern can restrict opportunities. Compare assignments under equivalent qualifications and examine the decision process. The description alone does not establish the manager’s complete emotional evaluation.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 2 · Objective 11.1a{{attrs[#blk-21o80w96jjmh]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 2
Unequal promotion rates identify one prejudiced decision maker: evaluate this claim.
:::
::: tab Answer
The disparity requires explanation but does not identify a person or mechanism. Examine eligibility, recruitment, evaluation criteria, informal access, and decisions across stages. Individual bias and institutional arrangements may both contribute.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 3 · Objective 11.2a{{attrs[#blk-0liqjxstbgfz]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 3
Participants give an ingroup member 6 tokens and an outgroup member 4. What comparison helps distinguish favoritism from derogation?
:::
::: tab Answer
Include a comparable recipient without the relevant group distinction. A neutral allocation of 4 is consistent with an ingroup bonus; a neutral allocation of 6 suggests an outgroup disadvantage. Neither pattern alone proves a hostile motive. The baseline must be comparable.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 4 · Objective 11.2a{{attrs[#blk-rwlcw1edl2d7]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 4
Would a minimal-group effect establish that history is irrelevant to discrimination?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. A mechanism can operate in a limited laboratory task while history, institutions, interests, and status shape real conflicts. Sufficiency in one task does not establish exclusive importance elsewhere.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 5 · Objective 11.3a{{attrs[#blk-lj9a2qp1h4zu]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 5
A program completes tasks for a disadvantaged group but excludes members from training and decisions. Why is counting help episodes insufficient?
:::
::: tab Answer
The amount of assistance does not reveal whether recipients gain capability or remain dependent. Assess control, opportunities, and recipient preferences. Support could be redesigned to expand training and participation. Evidence about effects is needed before assigning one motive to helpers.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 6 · Objective 11.3a{{attrs[#blk-sx7gcr409nem]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 6
Can a positive stereotype guarantee a benefit to its targets?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. A favorable trait attribution can justify restrictive expectations or roles. Positive tone and beneficial consequences are separate empirical questions.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 7 · Objective 11.4a{{attrs[#blk-g2mcf5jbl4kq]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 7
Employees accept an unequal policy but fear retaliation. Does compliance establish perceived legitimacy?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. Compliance can reflect constraints and costs. Measure legitimacy judgments separately and compare settings with different protections for dissent. Beliefs and opportunities may jointly shape action.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 8 · Objective 11.4a{{attrs[#blk-y5cqs2s1jr3f]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 8
How could individual mobility and collective action coexist?
:::
::: tab Answer
A member may seek advancement while organizing to change group conditions. Measure both behaviors and their timing. Mutually exclusive categories can conceal combined strategies.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 9 · Objective 11.5a{{attrs[#blk-3udq91sxegnu]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 9
Two communities oppose a memorial: one cites exclusion of its history; another fears resource loss. Why might one reassurance fail?
:::
::: tab Answer
Similar opposition can arise from different concerns. Funding reassurance may not address recognition; symbolic inclusion may not address costs. Measure both concerns and test targeted responses while allowing mixed motives.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 10 · Objective 11.5a{{attrs[#blk-g59fprpz7y37]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 10
Does lower collective guilt always indicate reconciliation?
:::
::: tab Answer
No. It could reflect repair, denial, disengagement, or changed interpretation. Assess acknowledgment, trust, conduct, and repair rather than treating one emotional change as decisive.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 11 · Objective 11.6a{{attrs[#blk-uyq5bvn74le9]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 11
A participant receives a personal label solely from one indirect-task score. What is wrong?
:::
::: tab Answer
The score describes performance under a procedure, with uncertainty and limited scope. It does not warrant a comprehensive personal label or establish discriminatory conduct. Assess reliability, validity, and the relevant behavioral prediction.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 12 · Objective 11.6a{{attrs[#blk-zkhj6mmnsabc]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 12
Matched applications receive 30% versus 20% callbacks in a hypothetical experiment. Interpret the difference.
:::
::: tab Answer
The difference is 10 percentage points. Under a well-controlled design it supports differential treatment associated with the manipulated cue in the sampled setting. It does not identify each employer’s full motive.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 13 · Objective 11.7a{{attrs[#blk-vpe9gx5f2hhi]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 13
A workshop improves liking but leaves unequal speaking time unchanged. What follows?
:::
::: tab Answer
It may improve the measured evaluation without demonstrating equal participation. Report outcomes separately, examine whose liking changed, and test participation rules. A partial benefit is not evidence of every intended effect.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
::::

## Question 14 · Objective 11.7a{{attrs[#blk-kz53w5h5n28a]}}

:::: tabs
::: tab Q 14
What risk arises when common identity requires ignoring subgroup experiences?
:::
::: tab Answer
It can obscure unequal conditions and specific harms. Evaluate inclusion alongside the ability to discuss differences and seek repair.

**Source basis:** Ellemers & Scheepers (2025).
:::
