## Module Summary

The chapter's title states its problem. Another person's mind is ultimately unknowable, and yet people manage
to make sense of each other constantly and well enough to act. The review is therefore about the wide variety
of what people *think* they can know — and it organizes that by **time**: what a perceiver brings before any
sensory input, what they extract in the first instant, what they decode moments later, what they infer across
repeated encounters, and what happens once both parties are doing all of this to each other at once.

The organizing move is deliberately data-driven rather than theory-driven, and it produces an unflattering
finding the authors state plainly: work on person perception has rarely been about *persons* — whole
autonomous individuals — or about *perceptions* in any strict sense. Perceivers mostly judge isolated cues;
the persons perceived get reduced to incomplete, fragmented, static chunks; and what is called perception is
really cognition and judgment. The continuum from broad to narrow has precedent in the contrast between
category-based and target-based knowledge, and in the distinction between automatic category-based
impressions and deliberate individuating ones.

The second organizing idea is that **accuracy is the wrong default goal**. Person perception looks more
driven by pragmatics than by getting the target right. This is not a minor caveat: it reframes the whole
enterprise. If perceivers are not trying to be accurate, then systematic departures from accuracy are not
necessarily failures — which connects this module directly to Module 1's rationality tension, and gives
students an early case where a "bias" may be a well-adapted process.

The chapter closes on the perceiver's opacity to themselves. People often remember an inference but not the
data that produced it; they cannot say why they find one face attractive or why they chose one option, and
they will construct plausible but irrelevant explanations instead. This is where the module's attribution
content bites, and where the seminar discussion should land.

## Core Dependency Chain

- **Person perception as making sense of the unknowable** — the target's mind is not directly accessible
  - This enables: the reframing of the field's goal from accuracy to pragmatics
  - This enables: the criterion problem — with no objective standard for the target's actual state, accuracy is hard even to define
- **Active perceivers shape the interaction**
  - This enables: understanding that social data are curated before they are processed, by both parties
  - This enables: the variance decomposition — accuracy may reside in the perceiver, the target, or their relationship
- **Baselines: pre-encounter assumptions**
  - This enables: everything downstream, since inference has already begun before any sensory input
  - This enables: intent → mind → the mentalizing stance applied even to non-human targets
- **Freeze-frame: demographic categories, warmth and competence**
  - This enables: approach or avoid, ingroup or outgroup → **Module 11**
  - This enables: the Stereotype Content Model's two-dimensional space
- **Real-time: decoding target states**
  - This enables: momentary mood and state inference from nonverbal and verbal cues → **Module 6**
- **Over time: trait inference under consistency, weight, and context**
  - This enables: target-based, individuating impressions at the far end of the continuum
- **Active targets and interdependent interaction**
  - This enables: the chapter's conclusion that the most significant context is two interdependent people
  - This enables: self-presentation and impression management → **Module 4**, **Module 10**
- **Perceiver opacity and correction failure**
  - This enables: the attribution material — anchoring on character and failing to correct for situation, including one's own contribution to it

## Section-Level Concept Map and Objectives

### 3.1 The miracle, and why accuracy is the wrong default

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain what "knowing the unknowable" names, and why the review is organized temporally.
- Articulate the criterion problem in accuracy research.
- Distinguish the three sources of variance in accuracy, and explain why separating them matters.

**Concept flow:**

- Person perception as a branch of social cognition; the target's mind ultimately unknowable
- The review's data-driven ambition: describe patterns of evidence people actually use, rather than starting from theory
  - The uncomfortable finding: rarely about persons as whole autonomous individuals, rarely about perception in the strict sense; perceivers mostly judge isolated cues
- Beyond accuracy as a goal
  - The criterion problem: what is the best indicator of the target's actual feelings or personality? Self-report cannot simply serve as the objective standard
  - Even with a standard, distance between standard and judgment does not capture the perceiver's ability
  - Three sources of variance: the perceiver reads minds well; the target is an open book; or perceiver and target resonate in a particular relationship
  - The critique that earlier research was doing the math wrong — and that the math said nothing about the perceiver's processes anyway

### 3.2 Baselines: what perceivers know before meeting

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain why person perception begins before sensory input.
- Trace the inference from intent to mind to mentalizing.
- Describe the valence and content of pre-encounter expectations.

**Concept flow:**

- Perceivers infer about the other as a human they are entitled to judge
- Perceivers seek the target's intent
  - Intent viewed as reflecting long-term behavioral tendencies and personality
  - Having intent implies having a mind
  - People do not see others as physical objects pushed around by the world; people are moved by their own thoughts, beliefs, desires and states
  - The intentional default is strong enough to be applied at times to non-human targets
  - Intent inferred from the target having a choice and attending to one alternative, especially a harder one
- Person perception's distinctive properties: the target is autonomous, an intentional causal agent trying to make an impression
  - Unobservable states and traits inferred from behavior — requiring a mentalizing imagination in the perceiver
- Pre-encounter expectations are both valenced (optimism) and contentful (humans have agency and complex emotions)

### 3.3 Freeze-frame: categories, warmth, and competence

**Learning objectives:**

- Describe what is extracted automatically in the first moment of an encounter.
- Explain the warmth and competence dimensions and what each signals.
- Connect the two-dimensional space to intergroup outcomes.

**Concept flow:**

- Demographic categories captured automatically: gender, race, class as societal identity
  - Spontaneous inferences from superficial cues to perceived group memberships
  - Simple trait inferences may follow from categorizing a behavior
  - The perceiver mind-reads from the outset, mainly deciding whether to approach as ingroup or avoid as outgroup
- Two dimensions
  - **Warmth / communality** — the target's intent, their interest in playing well with others
  - **Competence** — capability
  - They interact rather than operating independently
- The Stereotype Content Model's space
  - High warmth and competence together describe the ingroup and its allies; low on both describes society's most marginalized
  - Categories carry immediate messages of possibility: safety from warmth, dominance from competence
  - Perceived interdependence predicts perceived warmth

### 3.4 Real-time: decoding target states

**Learning objectives:**

- Describe what perceivers extract in the moments after categorization.
- Explain why state inference feels immediately accessible.
- Distinguish state inference from trait inference.

**Concept flow:**

- In the next instant after categorization, the target offers nonverbal and verbal clues to momentary state
  - Facial expression, posture, movement
  - Perceivers experience the inference as immediately accessible
  - Mind-reading is deeper here than in categorization
- States are momentary and rapidly sensed; traits require accumulation — the distinction organizes the rest of the chapter

### 3.5 Over time: traits, consistency, weight, and context

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain what distinguishes trait inference from earlier, faster processes.
- Describe the role of consistency across occasions.
- Locate trait inference on the category-based to target-based continuum.

**Concept flow:**

- What people learn across a series of encounters: more target-based traits, deliberately inferred
  - Inferred from scraps of an individual target's behavior in context, accumulated over time
  - Inferring trait dispositions should entail consistency of multiple observations over occasions
  - Note the exception: spontaneously categorizing what trait a behavior implies is an earlier, immediate inference
- Consistency, weight, and context govern how observations combine
- The continuum's far end: individuating, target-based knowledge rather than category-based

### 3.6 Not a lump: active targets and interdependent interaction

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain why the target cannot be modeled as a passive source of information.
- Describe self-presentation goals and their downside risks.
- Explain the chapter's conclusion about interdependence.

**Concept flow:**

- Targets continually, dyadically and dynamically shape the information they share; their goals may not align with the perceiver's
  - A person shows a different side of themselves to each group whose opinion they care about
- Self-presentation and impression management
  - The most powerful lever is choosing what to say and what to withhold
  - Five self-presentation goals — being liked, respected, admired, feared, helped — each with an enactment and a downside risk
  - Ingratiation risks appearing inauthentic; mere flattery backfires
- Interaction: both parties are perceiver and target simultaneously, and interdependently
  - Emotions change in response to others' emotions; disclosure depth matches what was received
  - Mere social presence changes thought, feeling, behavior and cognitive process
  - Objective self-awareness: mirrors, cameras and audiences make people self-aware, typically feeling inadequate against an ideal standard
- The perceiver's opacity to themselves
  - People often remember the inference but not the data that produced it
  - People cannot explain why they prefer one face or one option, and construct plausible but irrelevant explanations
  - People may infer their own emotions and preferences from observing their own choices, as an external observer would
- Attribution and correction
  - Anchoring on a character judgment, then correcting toward the situation
  - People are often unaware they fail to correct, and especially neglect their own influence as part of the situation
  - Correction can be curtailed when time or effort is short

## Common Student Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Conceptual repair |
|---|---|
| Evaluating every process against an accuracy standard | The chapter's claim is that person perception is driven more by pragmatics than by accuracy. Ask what the inference is *for* before asking whether it is right. |
| Assuming accuracy is easy to measure once you decide to | Return to the criterion problem: there is no straightforward objective standard for the target's actual state, and self-report will not serve as one. |
| Treating warmth and competence as independent checkboxes | The dimensions interact, and their combinations map onto distinct social positions rather than forming a simple scorecard. |
| Collapsing states into traits | States are momentary and rapidly sensed; traits require consistency across occasions. The temporal difference is the organizing principle of the chapter. |
| Treating the target as a passive information source | Targets curate what they share, with goals that may cut against the perceiver's. Impression management is part of the data, not noise in it. |
| Assuming people know why they judged as they did | People often retain the inference and lose the data, and will supply plausible but irrelevant explanations. This is why self-report about one's own social inference is weak evidence. |
| Reading the fundamental attribution error as simple carelessness | The correction process runs out of awareness and can be curtailed by limited time or effort — and perceivers especially neglect their *own* contribution to the situation. |
